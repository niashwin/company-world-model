A company is one world that many systems observe, but no system records the situation itself. We learn a state space that places every record, from email, chat, the CRM, tickets, calls or issues, by the situation that it describes. JEPAs trained on good views converge to one representation up to a rotation (the Platonic Representation Theorem). Organizations already keep good views: records that different systems link to the same customer in the same time window. We use a simulated company from Era by Eon, with 21,972 linked records and exact ground truth. Small JEPAs trained on these links place 76% of records with hidden links with the right customer, against 53% for TF-IDF. The same views raise a frozen pretrained encoder from 53% to 62%. About half of the signal remains when we mask every customer name and employee name. Access control is part of the model: planted secrets leak through an encoder trained on everything, even when we filter search. They do not leak when each reader's model trains only on what that reader may read. Memory should find records through the state space and read them from the store. Some questions come with one record of the customer instead of its name. With the names kept in the records, placement in the state space answers 63.8%, against 54.9% with a pretrained encoder and 68.7% with the record's own link. Dynamics did not emerge, even with the company's logged actions. A month's actions add nothing beyond the month's state. The company intervened in 25 of 29 months in trouble, in situations unlike those where it did not. So simulated decisions show where the company acted, not the effect of the action. Every encoder and head that we timed trains in 2-7 minutes on a CPU. We release the code, the data exporter and all results.
