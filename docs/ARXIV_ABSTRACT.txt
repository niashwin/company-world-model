A company is one world observed by many systems, yet no system records the situation itself. We learn a state space that places every record, from email, chat, CRM, tickets, calls or issues, by the situation it describes. JEPAs trained on good views converge to one representation up to a rotation (the Platonic Representation Theorem), and organizations already keep good views: records that different systems link to the same customer in the same window. On a simulated company from Era by Eon (21,972 linked records, exact ground truth), small JEPAs trained on these links place 76% of records whose links were hidden with the right customer, against 53% for TF-IDF; the same views raise a frozen pretrained encoder from 53% to 62%, and about half the signal survives masking every customer and employee name. Access control is built in: planted secrets leak through an encoder trained on everything even when search is filtered, and do not leak when each reader's model sees only what they may read. Beyond the state space we find a division of labor. Memory should route through the state space and read from the store: given one record of a customer instead of its name, placing it in the state space answers 63.8% of questions, against 54.9% with a pretrained encoder and 68.7% with its own link. Dynamics did not emerge, even conditioned on the company's logged actions: a month's actions add nothing beyond the month's state, which in the link-trained space already encodes most of them, and simulated decisions reflect the situations in which the company acted rather than the effect of acting, since it intervened in 25 of 29 troubled months, in situations unlike those where it held back. Every encoder and head we timed trains in 2-7 minutes on a CPU. We release code, data exporter and results.
