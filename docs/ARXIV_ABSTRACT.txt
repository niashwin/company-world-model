A company is one world observed by many systems, yet no system records the situation itself. We learn a state space that places every record, from email, chat, the CRM, support tickets, calls, calendars or issues, by the situation it describes. The Platonic Representation Theorem says that JEPAs trained on good views, different observations of one state, converge to one representation up to a rotation, and organizations already keep good views: records that different systems link to the same customer in the same window. On a simulated company from Era by Eon (21,972 linked records, exact ground truth), small JEPAs trained on these links place 76% of records whose links were hidden with the right customer, against 53% for TF-IDF; the same views raise a frozen pretrained encoder from 53% to 62%, and about half the signal survives masking every customer and employee name. Separately trained encoders align partly by one rotation. Access control is built in: planted secrets leak through an encoder trained on everything even when search is filtered, and do not leak when each reader's model sees only what they may read. Beyond the state space the experiments point to a division of labor. In memory, the state space should route a question to its situation and the stored records supply the detail: given one record of a customer instead of its name, placing that record in the state space and reading its customer's records answers 63.8% of questions, against 54.9% when a pretrained encoder places it, 52.2% with no store and 68.7% with the record's own link. Dynamics do not emerge from a space trained for static similarity: forecasts, linear dynamics, drift and cross-team distances stay at chance or simple baselines. We release code, data exporter and results.
