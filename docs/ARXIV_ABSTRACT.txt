Every company records its work in many systems, such as email, chat, customer records and support tickets. Each system holds part of what happens with a customer, but no system holds the situation itself. People rebuild that situation in their heads, and AI assistants rebuild it from documents for each question. A world model of the organization would hold that situation, as world models of physical scenes hold their state. Here we show that the links that enterprise systems keep can train the base of such a model: one state space for a company. A recent theorem predicts that encoders trained to predict one observation of a state from another converge to one representation. Records that different systems link to the same customer in the same period are such observations. We test this in a simulated company from Era by Eon, with 21,972 linked records and exact ground truth. There, small encoders trained on these links place 76% of records with withheld links with the right customer. Text similarity places 53%, and a guess of the largest customer 12%. With every name masked, they place 33%. The same links raise a frozen pretrained encoder from 53% to 62%. Permissions are part of the model: a reader's results do not change when we plant secrets in records that the reader may not read. The same space indexes a memory and a first decision layer. Each model trains in minutes on an ordinary computer. A company can thus learn a model of itself from its own records. We release the code, the data exporter and all results.
